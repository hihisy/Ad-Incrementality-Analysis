The ads paid for themselves. Almost half the impressions bought no measurable lift.

TaskaBella needed to determine how many handbag purchases its display ads generated beyond organic word-of-mouth demand. Rocket Fuel randomly assigned of users to the ad and the remaining to a public service announcement (PSA) in the same placement. This readout quantifies incremental purchases and ROI, evaluates the size of the control group, and proposes two follow-up experiments.

Reanalysis of UC Berkeley Haas case data. The experiment was run by Rocket Fuel and TaskaBella, Nov 2015 to Feb 2016.

What holds up and what doesn't

    A 4% holdout could detect a relative lift and cost

    The smallest lift each control share could detect with 80% power at a two-sided 5% level, for this campaign's 588,101 users.

    The observed lift line is known only after the test, so the crossing point cannot be used to size a holdout.

    Why not shrink it

    Each percentage point of control share gives up in incremental value. The holdout also makes segment analysis possible: at 4% the 200+ band has about control users, and its lift interval is already wide. At 1% it would have about , and that interval would be roughly twice as wide.

    Recommendation: keep 4% for the next campaign, since both follow-up tests below lean on segment-level comparisons.

    Methods and limitations

    Methods
    • Intention-to-treat comparison; pooled two-proportion z-test; unpooled 95% Wald intervals.
    • Sample ratio check against the planned 4% (chi-square goodness of fit) and balance checks on impression band, top-exposure day, and top-exposure hour (chi-square test of independence).
    • Holm correction within each family of comparisons (13 bins, 4 bands, 7 days, 16 hours) and Cochran's Q for differences in lift across groups.
    • Minimum detectable effect by control share at 80% power, two-sided 5%.
    • Cap design: trigger-based analysis and a non-inferiority margin equal to the break-even conversion loss. Bid design: intention-to-treat profit comparison with cost measured in the test.
    Limitations
    • Reanalysis of case data; the case notes that some names and figures were altered.
    • $40 per conversion and $9 CPM are case assumptions. ROI intervals reflect sampling uncertainty in the lift only.
    • Impression count, day, and hour are measured after assignment and differ between groups, so results split by them describe who the ad worked for, not causal effects of frequency or timing.
    • Impression bands were chosen after seeing the data.
    • Both follow-up experiments are designs; neither was run.
